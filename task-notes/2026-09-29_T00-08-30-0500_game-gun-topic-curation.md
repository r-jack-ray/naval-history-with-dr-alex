# Game gun topic curation

Request: remove generic gun-calibre topics supported only by game choices and lacking a historical connection through production, experimentation or a design proposal. Keep identifiable fictional weapons in `fiction-*`. Apply the user's broad historical criterion to real land and naval artillery, including conventional rounded metric equivalents.

## Coverage and decisions

Inventoried all 178 registry slugs ending in `-guns`: 101 numeric topics and 77 nonnumeric topics. Four parallel read-only reviews covered the full family; integration and shared commands are serialized. Reviewed current authored contexts, targeted literal transcript passages, registry metadata and applicable normalization rules. The initial usage report had 29,978 topics, 2,176 videos, 29,930 used topics, 48 unused topics, zero unregistered topics, 237 advisory similarity candidates, zero normalization blockers and zero review findings. The companion normalization-review report contained only its header.

The two explicitly fictional `-guns` names, `fiction-halo-mac-guns` and `fiction-wave-motion-guns`, already have the correct namespace. Generic gauss, electromagnetic, electrothermal-chemical and light-gas gun topics concern real engineering concepts and remain valid.

### Sources and rejected false positives

- [US National Military and Naval Museum inventory](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-02812_00_00-436-2084-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-02812_00_00-436-2084-0000.pdf), printed pp. 50-51: 2-inch and 3.4-inch weapons.
- [NHHC DANFS, UC-97](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/u/uc-97.html): 3.4-inch armament.
- [MoMA, Hugo van Werden's 1867 Krupp photograph](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/176892): nominal 36 cm coastal gun.
- [Alfred Watkins, Roaring Meg, Woolhope Transactions 1918-20](https://www.woolhopeclub.org.uk/system/files/documents/transaction/woolhope-club-1918-20.pdf), printed pp. 172-174: physical bore measurements and contemporary siege correspondence.
- [USNTMJ O-45(N), Japanese Naval Guns and Mounts, February 1946](https://www.fischer-tropsch.org/primary_documents/gvt_reports/USNAVY/USNTMJ%20Reports/USNTMJ-200F-0384-0445%20Report%20O-45%20N.pdf): experimental 19-inch/45 guns; introduction identifies Yamato/Musashi weapons as 18-inch/45, rather than the game's 46-calibre selection.
- [Italian Navy, Italia](https://www.marina.difesa.it/noi-siamo-la-marina/pilastro-operativo/mezzi/mezzi-storici/Pagine/EFGHI/italia.aspx): 431/27 mm battery.
- [US Army, Rodman Gun](https://www.army.mil/article/220158/ria_self_guided_tour_the_rodman_gun): actual 20-inch weapons.
- [Geraet 36 archival drawing and references](https://navweaps.com/Weapons/WNGER_21-52_gerat36.php): evidence for a historical 21-inch experiment.
- [Fisher, Records (1919), chapter XIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/69497/pg69497-images.html): 20- and 22-inch proposals.
- [Hudson Maxim, The Naval Battle of the Future (1909)](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/periodicals/NZGRAP19090908.2.61) and [US official appendix](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-03725_00_00-019-0021-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-03725_00_00-019-0021-0000.pdf): 24-inch proposal.
- [US Army TM 9-1985-3](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/ref/TM/pdfs/TM9-1985-3-German.pdf), printed p. 529, uses 35.3 cm projectile nomenclature. [Picatinny Arsenal PATR-2510](https://www.bulletpicker.com/pdf/PATR-2510.pdf), printed Ger258, distinguishes the 355 mm Howitzer M1 bore from its 35.3 cm ammunition. A projectile label does not establish a separate 13.9-inch bore family. PATR-2510 also gives an erroneous 12.397-inch conversion alongside 310 mm; 310 mm is about 12.205 inches and cannot establish a 12.4-inch family.

Other rejected search hits confused barrel length with bore, combined a quantity with a calibre (twelve 4-inch guns), misreported Borodino's 12-inch battery as 12.4 inches, or converted French 340 mm incorrectly to 13.9 inches. These do not provide a sound basis to retain the game-only topics. Some large primary PDFs were available through indexed text only; no visual inspection of unavailable pages is claimed.
