# Site Content Auditor

Use this brief with `$naval-site-content-auditor` for high-effort follow-up passes that make the public study guide richer, more searchable, and less mechanical. Use a high-accuracy single-agent runtime with high or greater reasoning effort. Keep model-version selection in the invoking runtime or automation rather than pinning it here.

Treat the audit as repeatable, but track saturation per model and effort level. Pass count does not prove completeness. If a pass only rephrases or rearranges existing material without adding transcript-backed substance, stop repeating that configuration; a materially stronger model, higher effort, improved method, or new evidence can justify another independent full-transcript comparison later.

Writing the audit outcome to `src/derived/site-content-processing.log` is mandatory for every audit attempt with an explicitly selected canonical shard path. Before handing back, append exactly one truthful row even if reading, editing, validation, permissions, or synchronization encounters an unresolved issue. Failed or skipped gates block successful completion, never outcome logging. Apply this requirement to every early exit.

## Mission

- Strengthen thin segment notes into useful study-guide prose.
- Reduce routine speaker attribution and mechanical narration in public-facing fields and evidence notes.
- Validate that expanded text stays grounded in transcript evidence.
- Preserve the segment-first model: `chapter`, `notable_point`, `qa`, and optional `transcript_excerpt`.

## Site Intent

- Write for someone who wants to learn naval history or general history and understand how armed forces, states, institutions, technology, and logistics work, not for another YouTube creator or maintainer.
- Each segment should act as a pointer to a useful source-video moment: what the viewer will encounter, what historical subject it illuminates, and why it is worth opening. Explain a naval connection when one matters, but do not require one.
- Include substantive general history on its own merits, including ancient, medieval, early-modern, and modern political, diplomatic, economic, social, technological, industrial, aviation, military, transport, and logistical history.
- Include historically informative games, simulations, and counterfactuals when they illuminate geography, strategy, operations, state capacity, economics, diplomacy, institutions, logistics, or interpretation. Distinguish scenario rules and events from actual history, and omit technical setup, personal chatter, bare gameplay narration, or other filler that adds no historical learning value.
- Favor precise, separate segments when the transcript supports them, so search and topic pages can send readers to exact moments.
- Keep search breadth in mind by naming ships, classes, navies, states, empires, peoples, armies, aircraft, battles, campaigns, leaders, weapons, railways, ports, places, trade routes, supply systems, policies, doctrine, logistics, acronyms, and common alternate phrasing when evidence supports it.
- Let significant topic slugs arise from transcript-backed content rather than a fixed taxonomy or tag quota. Preserve useful specificity in the per-video shard and complete deterministic registry synchronization in the same audit run before recording success.

## Wording Rules

- Review every segment's `title`, `summary`, `body`, `question`, `answerShort`, and `evidence[].note`. The wording checker includes evidence notes and reports their indexes, but a passing strict check does not resolve the contextual review candidates. Keep unrelated page and UI files outside the selected-shard scope. Follow the skill's [Final Segment-Wording Pass](skills/naval-site-content-auditor/SKILL.md#final-segment-wording-pass) for the editorial completion criteria.
- Every segment already carries `sourcePath` and `evidence`. Remove routine host attribution and generic reporting frames from public prose and evidence notes. Judge the need for names within each segment; a Bruships title or guest elsewhere does not exempt the shard. Preserve names needed for competing views, quotations, personal testimony, or other historical people. Identify each `host-attribution` match from the transcript and preserve other people named Clark or Clarke. Preserve confidence, uncertainty, and claim ownership while making the subject lead the sentence.
- Do not expose maintainer language such as "first pass", "later extraction", "processing", "curation", "source window", "evidence window", "search metadata", "seed", "prototype", "this segment exists to", or "useful for search" when it describes the site or content pipeline. The same words are allowed when the related transcript specifically uses them in the same subject-matter sense, such as warship prototypes, electoral first-past-the-post discussion, or data processing.
- Use "transcript passage" or "source passage" for visible source labels.
- Keep incomplete-work status in logs, reports, task notes, or handoff text.
- Avoid public YouTube analytics or internal identifiers unless the user asks for an admin/debug surface.

## Substance Rules

- A good `body` usually has 4-10 compact sentences.
- Name the subject directly: ship, navy, aircraft, army, battle, campaign, class, policy, transport or logistical system, technical system, or strategic problem.
- Explain the takeaway and learning value, not just why the record exists.
- For `qa`, make `answerShort` the direct answer and use `body` for reasoning, limits, and context.
- If the transcript evidence does not support a meatier note, inspect the cited time range before expanding. Do not pad with generic topic language.

## Audit Workflow

1. Require an explicitly named exact per-video shard. If none was supplied, stop without edits; do not select from the shard directory.
2. Read that shard, then read the transcript TXT named by its `sourcePath`. Treat the TXT as the historical source of record and the exact supplied shard as the only owned public-content file.
   - Keep transcript reads antivirus-safe. Use separate simple commands with a literal transcript path and fixed numeric `Get-Content | Select-Object -Skip/-First` slices; use `rg -n --fixed-strings` to locate a known timestamp when useful.
   - Do not build inline PowerShell multi-range timestamp extractors, stream the entire transcript through regex-heavy `ForEach-Object` or `foreach` logic, encode or move equivalent dynamic logic into an ad hoc script, or retry an endpoint-protection block in another form.
3. Scan only the selected shard for short bodies, repeated attribution, source/answer reporting frames, watch-point filler, and workflow wording, including evidence notes. Apply Humanizer to individual segments or small batches, reading each segment's fields together. Complete coverage of the shard without one overloaded whole-file rewrite. Preserve each evidence note's local support and limitations. Re-scan all fields after editing and justify retained references; passing the wording checker does not waive this editorial gate.
4. Edit only the exact supplied `src/derived/video-segments/<manifest.fileStem>.json` shard during an ordinary content audit. Do not derive or substitute a different shard path from metadata. Keep `videoId` at the shard root and do not add it to segment records. Preserve every other shard and all generated outputs. Do not inspect or manually edit `topics.json`; the deterministic synchronizer is the only permitted shared-source writer.
5. Add omitted segments and significant topic slugs revealed by the deeper read. Preserve substantive general history whether or not it connects to a navy or ship. For games, simulations, and counterfactuals, capture historically informative analysis with clear real-history-versus-scenario caveats rather than excluding the video's main subject or presenting game events as historical fact. Resolve active creation rules before the canonical write, and treat review, ambiguous, or synchronization failures caused by this shard as blockers to successful completion. Resolve them within scope when possible; otherwise record them in the mandatory audit-log row and handoff.
6. Read `src/derived/topic-normalization-patterns.tsv` before evaluating topic arrays and follow it as the detailed steady-state naming source. Resolve new slugs through active `creation` rules and preserve established slugs unless the active creation policy canonicalizes them. Leave `review`, disabled, ambiguous, or inapplicable candidates unchanged and report them; never edit the catalog or perform corpus-wide topic rewrites from this shard-only workflow.
7. Use `fiction-...` topics for referents that exist only inside a fictional work, not for counterfactual real history, real proposed or unbuilt designs, possible future systems, or genre/format topics. When a fictional illustration carries a real-world lesson, retain both layers: the fictional referent and ordinary transcript-backed topics for the real doctrine, engineering, logistics, institution, or other lesson.
8. Record precise remaining ranges when more substance can still be extracted. On every content-exhaustion review, compare the full transcript against the current shard rather than limiting inspection to existing segment windows. Stop when that model-and-effort configuration produces churn without new transcript-backed substance, and preserve eligibility for a later review under a materially stronger configuration or improved method.
9. After transcript comparison and draft preparation, apply every justified change directly to the selected shard. Run `C:\Program Files\nodejs\npm.cmd run check:site-content-wording -- --path <canonical shard path> --strict --review`. Fix every high-confidence issue, inspect every review candidate against the transcript, and rerun the wording check until it succeeds; preserve legitimate subject-matter wording and do not require review candidates to reach zero. Routine solo-speaker attribution and replaceable reporting frames must be cleared from public fields and evidence notes. Retain checker candidates only for concrete transcript-grounded reasons such as another person's surname, a quotation, or personal testimony needing an owner. Run this scoped check even for an unchanged, saturated, or intentionally empty completed audit. After every wording repair is written, run `bun run ./src/scripts/sort-video-segments-by-start.ts <canonical shard path>` once with `sandbox_permissions: require_escalated` on the first attempt, then validate the canonical shard. Continue after `Sorted` or `Already sorted` without rerunning the sorter. Rerun it only after repairing a malformed timestamp, sorter failure, or canonical-validation finding that demonstrates an actual chronological-ordering problem. Then run `C:\Program Files\nodejs\npm.cmd run sync:video-topics` with `sandbox_permissions: require_escalated` on the first attempt; do not try it inside the sandbox first. Run synchronization even for an unchanged, saturated, or intentionally empty completed audit. If an unresolved issue stops this sequence, skip dependent operations and record which required steps did not run. Do not run generation, repository-wide audits or checks, tests, Astro/Pagefind builds, or any shared command other than the scoped wording check and topic synchronization.
10. Always append exactly one newline-terminated result row at the physical bottom of `src/derived/site-content-processing.log` before handing back, after the required gates succeed or when an unresolved issue stops the audit. Include incomplete, blocked, failed, unchanged, saturated, and intentionally empty outcomes. Log reading, wording, sorting, validation, topic, elevation, and synchronization problems, including blockers in other shards. Use the skill's existing four-field format and 19-character local timestamp. State the actual changes, coverage, failed stage and reason, and later steps not run; report success only when all required gates passed. Retries within one audit attempt produce one outcome row. Verify the log header and preserve all earlier rows. If writing the log itself fails, use the skill's narrow recovery instructions, check for an already-written row before retrying an uncertain append, and report the exact pending row and log-write blocker if writing remains impossible. Never omit a row because another audit step failed or wait for the user to request it.
11. Read-only inspection scoped to the owned shard and its transcript TXT is allowed when useful. A lane-specific automation may additionally run only the private temporary-directory checks explicitly provided by that automation prompt.
12. Report the audited shard, whether it changed, strengthened content, topic slugs introduced, active creation rules used, unresolved review or ambiguous candidates, the scoped wording-check result, public-field and evidence-note attribution counts before and after, and reasons for retained transcript-supported references, the sorter and canonical-validation outcomes, whether synchronization ran with elevation and its corpus-wide result, the processing-log line appended, remaining thin ranges, and that the normalization catalog, manual topic-registry editing, corpus-wide topic rewriting, generation, other tests, builds, and validation were intentionally not touched. Identify failed or unrun steps accurately. If writing the log itself remains impossible, provide the exact pending row and log-write blocker and state that the row has not been written. Do not attribute every synchronized record to this shard.
